PR Pathways
The Complete Guide to Australian Permanent Residency in 2026

Australian Permanent Residency is one of the most life-changing milestones a person can achieve — and one of the most misunderstood. At JRM Migration, we work with clients every day who come to us confused, overwhelmed, and unsure where to even begin.

This guide is our way of cutting through the noise and giving you a clear, honest picture of what PR actually looks like in 2026.

What is Permanent Residency?

PR allows you to live, work, and study in Australia indefinitely — with no visa renewals and no expiry dates. You gain access to Medicare, the right to sponsor eligible family members, and after meeting residency requirements, the pathway to Australian citizenship.

The Main PR Pathways in 2026

1. Skilled Independent — Subclass 189
No employer or state sponsor required. Applications are submitted through SkillSelect based on a points score. A minimum of 65 points is required, though most successful applicants score 80 or above.

2. Skilled Nominated — Subclass 190
A state or territory nominates you, adding 5 points to your score. Generally more accessible than the 189, with a commitment to residing in the nominating state for a minimum of two years.

3. Employer Sponsored — Subclass 482 to 186
Your employer sponsors you on a temporary skilled visa first. After two years of employment, you become eligible to apply for permanent residency.

4. Student Visa to PR
Study in Australia, graduate, transition to a graduate visa, secure skilled employment, then apply for a skilled migration visa. A well-established pathway for thousands every year.

How the Points Test Works

  • Age 25 to 32 years: 30 points
  • Superior English proficiency: 20 points
  • Australian skilled work experience: up to 20 points
  • Bachelor, Masters or Doctorate degree: 15 to 20 points
  • Study in regional Australia: 5 points
  • Single applicant: 10 points

Student Visas
Student Visa to PR — Is It Possible?

Every year, thousands of Indian students arrive in Australia with a dream — not just to study, but to build a life here. The good news is that the pathway from student visa to permanent residency is real, achievable, and well-travelled.

At JRM Migration, we have helped many students understand exactly how this journey works. Here is what you need to know.

Step 1 — Student Visa (Subclass 500)

Everything begins with your student visa. You enrol in a registered course, meet the genuine temporary entrant requirement, and demonstrate sufficient funds. Once approved, you study, work part-time, and begin building your life in Australia.

Step 2 — Graduate Temporary Visa (Subclass 485)

After graduation, most international students qualify for the Temporary Graduate visa. This gives you 2 to 6 years to live and work in Australia — depending on your qualification and where you studied.

Step 3 — Build Your Points and Skills

While on your graduate visa, this is your most important window. Work in your nominated occupation, improve your English score, and accumulate Australian work experience — all of which add points toward a skilled visa.

Step 4 — Apply for a Skilled Visa

With enough points and an occupation on the skilled list, you submit an Expression of Interest through SkillSelect. When invited, you apply for either the Subclass 189 or Subclass 190 — both lead directly to permanent residency.

Our Honest Advice

The student to PR pathway takes planning — the right course, the right occupation, the right location. Starting early makes all the difference. Book a free consultation with our team and we will map out your personal pathway from day one.

Visa Tips
5 Mistakes That Get Visa Applications Refused

Visa refusals are more common than people think — and the hardest part is that most of them are avoidable. At JRM Migration, we review applications every day and we see the same mistakes coming up again and again.

Mistake 1 — Incomplete or Incorrect Documents

Missing a single document or submitting an outdated version can lead to an immediate refusal. Every visa has a specific checklist — and every document must be current, certified, and in the correct format.

Mistake 2 — Poor English Test Scores

Many applicants underestimate how important IELTS or PTE scores are. A difference of one band can mean 10 fewer points — which could determine whether you receive an invitation at all.

Mistake 3 — Incorrect Occupation or Skills Assessment

Choosing the wrong ANZSCO occupation code, or using the wrong assessing authority, can invalidate your entire application. This is one of the most technical aspects of migration — and where professional guidance makes the biggest difference.

Mistake 4 — Not Disclosing Previous Visa Refusals

Australian immigration authorities have access to your full visa history. Failing to disclose a previous refusal — even from another country — is considered a serious character issue and can result in a permanent ban.

Mistake 5 — Using an Unregistered Migration Agent

In Australia, only MARA registered agents are legally allowed to provide migration advice. Always check the MARA register before engaging anyone.
